## 1. Literature curation: scope, quality, and backchecking

### 1.1 Scope and problem type

This report is limited to postoperative abdominal/peritoneal adhesion prevention. Intrauterine, pleural, tendon, epidural, and cell-culture anti-adhesion studies are not merged into the same conclusion. The problem is `MIX`:

- **WHY**: How far have physical barriers, antifouling interfaces, and inflammatory/oxidative regulation been demonstrated, and where does the causal chain remain unproven?
- **HOW**: How could an interface cover the injury, suppress non-target tissue bridging during the critical repair window, and exit when it is no longer needed?

### 1.4 What the backcheck changes

The backcheck weakens an overly broad narrative: **anti-adhesion is not an untouched problem**. C4 reports that Seprafilm did not significantly improve overall bowel obstruction, while adhesive small-bowel obstruction requiring reoperation was reduced. C3 likewise notes that clinical barriers can reduce adhesion formation, while evidence for fertility, pain, or overall obstruction outcomes remains limited. C5 reports statistical equivalence between a polysaccharide film and Seprafilm in a rat peritoneal abrasion model.

The research gap is therefore narrowed to:

1. Whether dynamic interface state and residence window can be independently designed and validated;
2. How much physical separation, antifouling, and ROS/inflammation regulation each contribute;
3. How animal adhesion endpoints can be connected to clinically relevant outcomes without treating an animal score as clinical efficacy.

Evidence status: `Supported`. Higher-level or stronger comparative evidence was found, but this is still not a systematic review.

## 2. Core judgment and problem frame

### 2.1 One-sentence core judgment

The unresolved problem is not whether a hydrogel can reduce adhesion in animals. It is **how to turn coverage, tissue-side retention, cavity-side antifouling, local inflammatory regulation, and material exit into a measurable spatiotemporal interface state—and show that this state, rather than formulation complexity itself, determines the adhesion outcome**.

Evidence status: `Supported`; this is a synthesis of P1–P8 and C1–C5, not a direct claim from one paper.

## 3. First-principles causal model

### 3.1 Biological chain

```text
Surgical injury
  → fibrin deposition and increased ROS/inflammation
  → protein, inflammatory cells, and fibroblasts enter the injured interface
  → delayed mesothelial repair, insufficient fibrinolysis, and increased collagen/fibrosis
  → abnormal bridging between adjacent tissues
  → postoperative adhesion
```

C2, C3, and C7 support the broad relationships among injury, inflammation, fibrin, mesothelial repair, and fibrosis. The arrows do not have equal evidence strength; they are separated in the ledger below.

### 3.2 Intervention chain

```text
Hydrogel X
  → changes coverage, residence, interfacial chemistry, and responsive release
  → tissue separation + cavity-side antifouling + tissue-side retention + microenvironment regulation
  → less protein/cell bridging while mesothelial repair remains possible
  → lower animal adhesion phenotype Y
  → clinical benefit? [U]
```

## 5. Integrated Paper Analysis Cards

Each card keeps citation metadata, source role, WHY, HOW, WHAT, causal evidence, and limitation in one analysis unit.

### P1 — PECE thermoresponsive physical barrier

- **Citation metadata**: Yang B et al., *Preventing postoperative abdominal adhesions in a rat model with PEG-PCL-PEG hydrogel*, *International Journal of Nanomedicine* (2012), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2147/IJN.S26141).
- **Study type / model**: Original animal study; rat abdominal adhesion model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; early in-situ gelation and short-residence physical-barrier anchor.
- **Why included**: Early reference for the “rapid coverage–limited residence–mesothelial repair” window.
- **WHY**: Liquid barriers leave quickly, while solid films are difficult to apply to irregular wounds. The study actually tests whether thermoresponsive PECE can form a short-term barrier in one rat model.
- **HOW**: Sol–gel transition at 37 °C forms physical separation, followed by degradation and absorption.
- **WHAT**: Lower adhesion was observed. Gelation, residence/absorption, adhesion, and mesothelialization were measured. The authors interpret the result as reduced fibrin/fibroblast access. The model-level effect is `Demonstrated`; residence time itself is not isolated causally.
- **Causal evidence**: L3 `[D/C]`, L6 `[C/U]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: One animal model and limited mechanism separation; establishes a residence–repair baseline.

### P2 — Purely zwitterionic hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Guo Q et al., *In Situ Clickable Purely Zwitterionic Hydrogel for Peritoneal Adhesion Prevention*, *Chemistry of Materials* (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.0c00889).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; in-vitro protein/fibroblast adhesion and rat cecum–abdominal wall model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; cavity-side antifouling anchor.
- **Why included**: Separates the barrier concept into a low-protein/low-cell-adhesion interface state.
- **WHY**: A physical barrier alone may not prevent protein deposition, inflammation, and fibroblast attachment. The study tests whether an antifouling hydrogel reduces model adhesion.
- **HOW**: An in-situ clickable zwitterionic network changes interfacial hydration and nonspecific adhesion; antibiotics can be incorporated.
- **WHAT**: Lower protein/fibroblast adhesion and lower animal adhesion were observed and measured with inflammatory and blood-compatibility readouts. The antifouling-to-in-vivo bridge remains partly correlated.
- **Causal evidence**: L4 `[D/C]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Antifouling and network/drug effects are not fully factorized; establishes the cavity-side antifouling dimension.

### P3 — CFQ dynamic covalent self-healing hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Zeng H et al., *Self-healing, injectable hydrogel based on dual dynamic covalent cross-linking against postoperative abdominal cavity adhesion*, *Acta Biomaterialia* (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actbio.2022.08.030).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; mouse abdominal adhesion model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; dynamic network and multifunctional microenvironment anchor.
- **Why included**: Represents a complex system where self-healing, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial functions co-vary.
- **WHY**: Conventional barriers may not address infection, inflammation, and oxidative stress simultaneously. The actual test is the combined network in one mouse model.
- **HOW**: Carboxymethyl chitosan, 2-FPBA, and quercetin form a dual dynamic covalent network with injection, self-healing, release, and microenvironment effects.
- **WHAT**: Adhesion reduction and dynamic network behavior were observed. Release/residence, adhesion, TGF-β1, fibrinogen, TNF-α, IL-6, tPA/PAI-1, and related measures were reported. The combination is `Supported`; the self-healing contribution is not isolated.
- **Causal evidence**: L2/L5 `[C]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Many modules and weak attribution; a canonical example for G2 factorization.

### P4 — Microgel-based cream hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Liu B et al., *Multifunctional Microgel-Based Cream Hydrogels for Postoperative Abdominal Adhesion Prevention*, *ACS Nano* (2023), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.2c12104).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; mouse cecum–abdominal wall model with proteomics.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; microgel structure, antioxidant activity, and molecular-readout anchor.
- **Why included**: Places interface structure, EGCG/HA/PVA chemistry, and proteomic changes in one system.
- **WHY**: Irregular wounds require coverage, while a single barrier may not address inflammation and oxidative stress. The actual test is a multifunctional cream hydrogel in mice.
- **HOW**: EGCG–HA microgels and PVA form an injectable/self-healing dynamic boronate network.
- **WHAT**: Lower inflammation, oxidative stress, fibrosis, and adhesion were observed. Antioxidant, cell-adhesion, histology, and label-free proteomics were measured. Proteomic association is not a causal target demonstration.
- **Causal evidence**: L4/L5 `[C]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Independent blockade of proteomic targets is missing; demonstrates why molecular readout is not automatically mechanism.

### P5 — ROS-responsive sprayable hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Huang Y et al., *ROS-responsive sprayable hydrogel as ROS scavenger and GATA6⁺ macrophages trap for the prevention of postoperative abdominal adhesions*, *Journal of Controlled Release* (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2024.03.051).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; rat sidewall defect–cecal abrasion model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; state response, sprayability, and immune-regulation anchor.
- **Why included**: Treats local ROS as a lesion-state input rather than using only fixed release.
- **WHY**: Fixed release and static barriers may not match the postoperative ROS/inflammatory trajectory. The actual test is a ROS-responsive sprayable system in animals.
- **HOW**: A dynamic covalent network gels rapidly; high ROS triggers EGCG release; sulfonate groups alter protein/cell adhesion.
- **WHAT**: A coverage layer and lower adhesion were observed. Gelation, shear thinning, self-healing, wet adhesion, ROS, macrophage, and fibrinolysis-related readouts were measured. Feasibility is `Supported`; contribution and exit timing remain unresolved.
- **Causal evidence**: L4/L5 `[D/C]`, L6 `[U]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Spatial direction is not fully separated; motivates state-variable-driven switching.

### P6 — nMGel sprayable nanoparticle–microgel system

- **Citation metadata**: Chen J et al., *Adhesive Nanoparticle-in-Microgel System with ROS Scavenging Capability and Hemostatic Activity for Postoperative Adhesion Prevention*, *Small* (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/smll.202306598).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; mouse cecal defect model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; sprayable powder, hemostasis, and ROS-scavenging anchor.
- **Why included**: Adds wet, irregular wounds, spraying, and bleeding constraints to the design problem.
- **WHY**: Liquids or planar films may not cover and remain fixed on irregular wounds. The actual test is a sprayable nanoparticle–microgel system in mice.
- **HOW**: MnO₂ nanoparticles are loaded into gelatin microspheres and coated with polydopamine; the powder forms a hydrogel on contact with liquid.
- **WHAT**: Wound coverage and reduced adhesion were observed. Wet adhesion, hemostasis, antioxidant activity, inflammation, collagen, and angiogenesis were measured. Powder-format superiority over other delivery forms remains `[U]`.
- **Causal evidence**: L3/L5 `[D/C]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Limited model and delivery-form extrapolation; establishes the D8 operating constraint.

### P7 — Tough Janus PVA hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Lin X et al., *A tough Janus poly(vinyl alcohol)-based hydrogel for wound closure and anti postoperative adhesion*, *Acta Biomaterialia* (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actbio.2024.08.049).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; rat injury and cecum–peritoneum models.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; spatially asymmetric interface anchor.
- **Why included**: Directly addresses the conflict between fixation to the wound and non-adhesion to neighbouring tissue.
- **WHY**: One surface cannot easily maximize tissue adhesion and cavity-side anti-adhesion at once. The study tests whether Janus orientation separates the two functions.
- **HOW**: Tissue-side PAA/PEI adhesive layer, tough PVA/tannic-acid middle layer, and zwitterionic cavity-side anti-adhesion layer.
- **WHAT**: Wound fixation with lower cavity-side adhesion was observed. Toughness, wet interfacial strength, inflammation, and animal adhesion were measured. Dynamic reorientation and exit are not tested.
- **Causal evidence**: L4 `[D/C]`, L6 `[U]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Layer orientation, in-vivo stability, and degradation are translational constraints; provides the key logic for G3 and Idea 2.

### P8 — ROS-responsive double-network hydrogel

- **Citation metadata**: Ren L et al., *Engineering ROS-responsive double network hydrogel as bioactive barrier for postoperative abdominal adhesions prevention*, *Bioactive Materials* (2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bioactmat.2025.07.021).
- **Study type / model**: Original material/animal study; rat abdominal adhesion model.
- **Evidence tier / source role**: Tier B; long-residence, double-network, and ROS-cascade anchor.
- **Why included**: Represents the latest complex route combining stronger networks, longer residence, and ROS regulation.
- **WHY**: HA-like materials may clear too quickly, while simply extending residence may compromise repair. The actual test is the integrated PD-OHN effect in one rat model.
- **HOW**: A double network improves mechanics and wet adhesion; a ROS-responsive DPBA network scavenges ROS and releases DTT.
- **WHAT**: Lower adhesion and no obvious major-organ abnormality were reported. Modulus, tissue adhesion, roughly 21-day residence, adhesion score, M1/M2, tPA/PAI-1, collagen, and safety were measured. The 21-day window is not shown to be optimal.
- **Causal evidence**: L2/L5/L6 `[C]`.
- **Main limitation / contribution**: Complex attribution and limited clinical extrapolation; directly triggers G1/G3.

## 8. Solved and unsolved space

### Demonstrated

- P1–P8 report lower adhesion phenotypes in different rat/mouse postoperative models; some directly measure residence, antifouling, ROS/inflammation, or tissue repair.
- C4 shows that a clinical benchmark can have different effects on overall obstruction and reoperation adhesive obstruction; animal adhesion reduction is not the same endpoint as clinical benefit.
- C5 reports statistical equivalence between a polysaccharide film and Seprafilm in a rat model, showing that a more complex hydrogel is not the only possible effective route.

### Supported

- Physical separation is the common base; antifouling, ROS/inflammatory regulation, and spatial separation may add benefit under particular conditions.
- P7 supports spatially separating tissue-side retention from cavity-side anti-adhesion; P5/P8 support the feasibility of using ROS as a responsive input.
- The evidence ceiling of the core studies remains mainly small-animal adhesion endpoints, not long-term patient benefit.

### Inferred

- Low adhesion may depend more on an interface-state trajectory than on a single modulus, adhesion strength, antioxidant molecule, or residence time.
- The major unresolved issue in multifunctional formulations may be attribution and sequence, not a lack of functions.

### Speculative

- A hydrogel that switches function with injury state, spatial direction, and repair progress may outperform a static uniform barrier.
- The genuinely new point of a ROS-responsive Janus design would be state-coupled spatial switching, not simply combining the components of P5, P7, and P8.

## 9. Gap map

### G1 — Technical Gap: no comparable coverage–residence–exit window

- **Evidence**: P1, P3, and P8 report different residence/absorption times; P5/P6 address field coverage; C3 frames early mesothelial repair as clinically relevant.
- **Missing capability**: Independently tune residence under one interface chemistry while measuring material, mesothelium, and adhesion.
- **Why current evidence is insufficient**: Formulation, model, and modules all change together, so 7–21-day differences cannot be attributed to residence alone.
- **Minimal discriminator**: Same network/interface chemistry, matched initial coverage and mass, only 3/7/14/21-day exit timing changed.
- **Status**: `Inferred`.

### G2 — Knowledge Gap: barrier, antifouling, and microenvironment contributions are not factorized

- **Evidence**: P3–P6 and P8 jointly change physical barrier, ROS, inflammation, antibacterial, or fibrosis pathways.
- **Missing link**: Whether lower adhesion is driven mainly by less contact, less protein/cell adhesion, altered inflammation, or sequence.
- **Why current evidence is insufficient**: No matched residence/coverage with module ablation; proteomics, macrophage, and ROS changes do not automatically become causal mechanisms.
- **Minimal discriminator**: Barrier × cavity-side antifouling × ROS/anti-inflammatory module design with synchronized L4/L5/L6 readouts.
- **Status**: Cross-paper pattern `Supported`; dominant mechanism `Speculative`.

### G3 — Assumption Gap: stronger adhesion, higher modulus, and longer residence are not automatically better

- **Evidence**: P7 separates tissue-side adhesion and cavity-side anti-adhesion; P8 emphasizes long residence without showing 21 days is optimal; C3/C4 show phenotype–patient outcome decoupling.
- **Shared premise**: The stronger, tougher, and longer-lasting the material, the better the anti-adhesion effect.
- **Why insufficient**: The actual goal is an interface that prevents non-target bridging while supporting normal repair, not maximal retention.
- **Alternative prediction**: With fixation strength matched, cavity-side antifouling and exit timing may predict low adhesion better than total adhesion strength.
- **Minimal discriminator**: Match material amount, mechanics, and total residence; alter only orientation/antifouling; measure non-target adhesion, mesothelial repair, and adhesion.
- **Status**: `Speculative`, directly motivated by P7 and C3/C4 boundaries.

## 11. Non-combinatorial Idea Cards

### Idea 1 — Incremental: standardized residence-window matching

- **Type / operator**: Measurement / Material; Remove a Bottleneck + Explain an Anomaly.
- **Source gaps and evidence IDs**: G1; P1, P3, P8, C3; links L3/L6.
- **Unresolved causal link**: Does the material-state trajectory alter adhesion through mesothelial re-coverage?
- **Why existing studies cannot resolve it**: Residence, network chemistry, anti-inflammatory modules, and models change together.
- **Minimal state variable**: `T_interface / T_mesothelium`, the duration of effective interface coverage relative to mesothelial re-coverage.
- **Hypothesis**: Adhesion is lowest within a finite ratio window; too short misses early bridging, too long adds no benefit and may delay repair.
- **What is genuinely new**: Residence becomes a cross-material dimensionless variable, not a formulation description.
- **Non-combination novelty check**: Removing all material names leaves a time-matching rule, a window prediction, and falsification conditions.
- **Critical experiment**: Same network and cavity-side interface, only 3/7/14/21-day degradation changed; measure material, mesothelium, adhesion, and separation force.
- **Prediction / falsification**: A plateau or optimum window should align with mesothelial coverage. Falsify if all windows are equivalent or the longest residence always improves outcome without repair cost.
- **Ablation / controls**: Residence × antifouling 2×4 design; match initial mass, coverage, modulus, and procedure.
- **Value / feasibility / risk**: Technical/mechanistic; high feasibility; animal-to-human time-window mismatch remains a risk.

### Idea 2 — Integrative: state-coupled spatially adaptive interface

- **Type / operator**: Material / Mechanism; Resolve a Contradiction + Cross-scale Bridging.
- **Source gaps and evidence IDs**: G2/G3; P5, P7, P8, C3; links L4/L5/L6.
- **Unresolved causal link**: Can local injury state drive direction-specific interface switching and improve the repair–adhesion trade-off?
- **Why existing studies cannot resolve it**: P5 mainly tests ROS response, P7 spatial separation, and P8 long residence; none tests state input × spatial direction × exit under a common model.
- **Minimal state variable**: `Z(t)`, a local injury-state variable composed of ROS/fibrin/inflammatory signals that controls retention and exit kinetics.
- **Hypothesis**: Above an early-injury threshold, the interface maintains wound-side retention and regulation; as `Z(t)` falls, unnecessary residence decreases while cavity-side antifouling remains.
- **What is genuinely new**: `injury state Z → spatial function switching → state trajectory → adhesion outcome`, rather than a Janus + ROS component list.
- **Non-combination novelty check**: Removing material names leaves a new state variable, coupling relation, and threshold prediction. If response only increases release without changing orientation or exit, the idea fails.
- **Critical experiment**: Uniform static, static Janus, uniform ROS-responsive, and state-coupled ROS-responsive Janus groups, matched for initial coverage, mass, baseline modulus, and total residence.
- **Prediction / falsification**: State-coupled Janus should preserve early wound coverage, minimize cavity-side protein/cell adhesion, exit faster during mesothelial repair, and outperform static Janus. Falsify if matched static Janus is equivalent or switching causes delayed repair/toxicity.
- **Ablation / controls**: Spatial orientation × ROS response × residence 2³ design, retaining static Janus and uniform responsive controls.
- **Value / feasibility / risk**: Mechanistic, cross-scale, assumption-challenging; medium-high feasibility; orientation stability and threshold drift are risks.

### Idea 3 — Transformative: interface-state trajectory as the prediction target

- **Type / operator**: Measurement / Framework; Reverse the Causality + Cross-scale Bridging.
- **Source gaps and evidence IDs**: G3; P1, P4, P5, P7, P8, C4; links L4/L5/L6/L7.
- **Unresolved causal link**: Can early interface trajectories predict later adhesion and repair better than a single terminal material parameter?
- **Why existing studies cannot resolve it**: Most studies use terminal adhesion scores; residence, interface adhesion, ROS, repair, and adhesion are not bound in one time series.
- **Minimal state variable**: A preregistered `interface state index` containing coverage, cavity-side protein adhesion, ROS, macrophage state, mesothelial coverage, and material exit.
- **Hypothesis**: Low adhesion is determined by the trajectory `coverage → antifouling → microenvironment regulation → exit`; trajectory features predict day-14/28 adhesion better than terminal modulus or adhesion strength.
- **What is genuinely new**: The object of study becomes a measurable dynamic interface state, with a testable predictive gain beyond a terminal score.
- **Non-combination novelty check**: This is not a pile of correlated readouts; the index, temporal order, and independent validation set must be pre-defined.
- **Critical experiment**: Longitudinal 0/3/7/14/28-day measurements across multiple materials, with early trajectory predicting later adhesion and repair.
- **Prediction / falsification**: The trajectory should explain different repair quality at similar terminal adhesion scores and improve prediction. Falsify if a single terminal material parameter is equally predictive.
- **Ablation / controls**: Remove each index component in turn; use a held-out validation set and do not include adhesion score itself in the index.
- **Value / feasibility / risk**: Mechanistic/measurement/framework; medium-low short-term feasibility; overfitting and correlation stacking are risks.

## 12. Idea scoring and selection

| Idea | Novelty | Importance | Mechanistic depth | Testability | Feasibility | Leverage | Total | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Residence-window matching | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 26 | Best standardized baseline and early Go/No-Go experiment |
| 2. State-coupled spatial interface | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 26 | Preferred mechanism idea; not a component collage |
| 3. Interface-state trajectory | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 23 | Long-term framework with higher execution cost |

### Preferred idea

Select Idea 2, but do not begin with a maximally complex formulation:

1. Use Idea 1 to establish the baseline `T_interface / T_mesothelium` window;
2. Test spatial direction, ROS response, and residence with a minimal 2³ design;
3. Only proceed to formulation optimization if state coupling makes a prediction beyond static Janus.

Its value is not merely the score. It addresses the open L4–L6 links and can be falsified using static Janus, uniform responsive, and matched-residence controls.

## 13. Critical experiment and Go/No-Go

### Phase 1 — In-vitro/ex-vivo mechanism separation

| Module | Key readout | Causal purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage and gelation | Gelation time, coverage uniformity, wet integrity | Test D1/D8 rather than treating operability as mechanism |
| Tissue-side retention | Lap shear, cyclic shear, orientation retention | Test D4 and separate retention from excessive adhesion |
| Cavity-side antifouling | Protein, fibroblast, macrophage adhesion | Directly test L4 |
| ROS response | Scavenging, release, threshold, kinetics | Test whether `Z(t)` is an informative state variable |
| Mesothelial repair | Viability, migration, coverage, inflammation | Prevent low adhesion at the cost of delayed repair |
| Exit window | Mass loss, network integrity, tracer residence | Directly test L6 and G1 |

### Phase 2 — Common model and benchmark

Use a sidewall-defect–cecal-abrasion model and include a clinical benchmark/simple barrier. Compare at least:

1. Injury-only;
2. Uniform static physical barrier;
3. Static Janus;
4. Uniform ROS-responsive;
5. State-coupled ROS-responsive Janus.

Match initial coverage, material mass, baseline modulus, and injury severity. At days 3, 7, 14, and 28 measure residence, cavity-side protein/cell adhesion, ROS, macrophage state, mesothelial coverage, collagen/fibrosis, and adhesion incidence/area/strength. Do not write the animal result as clinical efficacy.

### Go / No-Go

- **Go**: After matching mass, coverage, mechanics, and residence, state-coupled Janus lowers adhesion relative to static Janus, does not delay mesothelial repair or increase toxicity, and changes at least one prespecified intermediate state readout in parallel.
- **Conditional Go**: Benefit appears only in a defined ROS/residence window; narrow the claim to a state-window mechanism rather than universal self-adaptation.
- **No-Go**: Static Janus is equivalent; response increases release without changing the state trajectory; or there is layer detachment, repair delay, organ toxicity, residual material, or increased adhesion.

## 14. Evidence ceiling and next smallest action

- **Evidence ceiling**: The core set is mainly Tier B small-animal original research, not clinical efficacy evidence.
- **Backcheck boundary**: Context papers show partial clinical barrier effects but incomplete alignment between patient outcomes and adhesion scores; animal endpoints cannot be directly promoted to clinical benefit.
- **Still needed**: Stronger head-to-head hydrogel studies, external validation across abdominal models, sterilization/storage/delivery evidence, long-term safety, and clinically relevant endpoints.
- **Next smallest action**: First compare 3/7/14/21-day residence under one interface chemistry; then run the minimal static-Janus versus state-coupled-Janus 2³ experiment.
